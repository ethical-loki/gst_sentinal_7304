# E-Way Bill System Regulatory Documentation, Technical Architecture & Statutory Guidelines

## 1. Applicability, Definition, Statutory Mandate & Thresholds
- Definition & Statutory mandate: Governed under Section 68 of the CGST Act, 2017 read with Rule 138 of the CGST Rules, 2017. An E-Way Bill (Form GST EWB-01) is an official electronic document generated on the common portal to track and authenticate the physical movement of goods prior to transit commencement.
- Consignment value threshold:
  - Inter-State movements: Mandatory for all consignments where the consignment value (taxable value + GST + cess, excluding exempt value) exceeds ₹50,000.
  - Intra-State movements: Standard central default is ₹50,000. However, individual States and Union Territories have issued state-specific notifications raising intra-state limits (e.g., ₹1,00,000 in Maharashtra, Gujarat, West Bengal, Bihar, and Delhi for specified goods).
- Mandatory E-Way Bill without threshold limits (₹0 threshold):
  - Inter-State job work: Movement of goods sent for job work from a principal located in one state to a job worker in another state.
  - Inter-State transport of handicraft goods: Movement of handicraft goods by dealers exempted from obtaining GST registration under Section 24.
- Causing movement: The E-Way Bill can be generated by the consignor (supplier), consignee (recipient), or the transporter carrying the goods.

## 2. Part-A and Part-B Slip Generation Rules (Form GST EWB-01 Architecture)
- Structure: The E-Way Bill document consists of two distinct sections: Part-A (transaction details) and Part-B (conveyance/transport details).
- Part-A slip generation: Furnished by the generator using the tax invoice, bill of supply, delivery challan, or bill of entry.
- Mandatory fields: Supplier GSTIN, Recipient GSTIN, Place of Dispatch PIN, Place of Delivery PIN, Document Number & Date, Value of Goods, HSN Code (goods only), and Reason for Transportation.
- Part-A Slip Creation: If only the Transporter ID (transporter_id) is supplied or provided without conveyance details, only Part-A of the E-Way Bill is generated. In this case, Transport Mode, Vehicle Type, Vehicle Number, and transport document number/date should remain null or not passed.
- Part-A slip status & 15-day window: A Part-A slip does not permit the physical transit of goods; moving goods with only Part-A generated is legally treated as moving goods without an E-Way Bill. The assigned transporter or generator is allowed up to 15 days from the generation of Part-A to enter Part-B vehicle details. If Part-B is not filled within 15 days, the Part-A slip automatically lapses.
- Part-B slip generation: Completes the E-Way Bill and makes it valid for transit.
  - Road transport: Mandatory Vehicle Number and Vehicle Type (Regular / Over Dimensional Cargo).
  - Rail, Air, or Ship: Mode of transport, Transport Document Number (Railway Receipt - RR, Airway Bill - AWB, Bill of Lading - BL), and Document Date.
- Intra-State 50 km exemption for Part-B: Updating Part-B is not mandatory when goods are transported over a distance of less than 50 km within the same State/UT:
  - From the consignor’s place of business to the transporter's hub for further transit.
  - From the transporter’s hub to the recipient’s final place of business.
- Goods vs. Services validation: An E-Way Bill can be generated only if at least one item's HSN corresponds to goods (Chapters 1 to 98). Pure service invoices (Chapter 99) and financial Debit/Credit Notes lacking underlying goods movement are strictly barred from E-Way Bill generation.

## 3. Blocked Taxpayers & EWB Blocking (Rule 138E)
- Default blocking: The GST system automatically blocks E-Way Bill generation facilities (both outbound and inbound) for taxpayers who fail to file their GST returns:
  - Non-filing of GSTR-3B returns for two or more consecutive tax periods (monthly/quarterly).
  - Non-filing of statement in CMP-08 for two or more consecutive quarters (Composition taxpayers).
  - Non-filing of GSTR-1 for two or more consecutive months/quarters.
- Unblocking mechanism:
  - Automatic unblocking: Occurs within 24 hours once all defaulted returns are successfully filed on the GST portal.
  - Manual unblocking: Jurisdiction Tax Officer can manually unblock the facility in Form GST EWD-03 upon an application submitted in Form GST EWD-02 under exceptional circumstances.

## 4. Distance Calculation & PIN-to-PIN Route Logic
- Auto-calculated PIN distance: The E-Way Bill system utilizes an embedded GIS mapping database to determine standard road transit distances based on source and destination PIN codes.
- Allowed distance tolerances:
  - General tolerance: The distance entered by the generator must fall within ±10% of the portal's auto-calculated PIN-to-PIN distance.
  - Distances below 100 km: When the auto-calculated distance is less than 100 km, the allowed entry is between 1 km and (Auto-distance + 10%).
  - Zero distance input: Passing distance = 0 via the API or web form prompts the system to automatically populate and lock the internal auto-calculated distance.
  - Same PIN code rule: When the source and destination PIN codes are identical, the system limits the permissible distance strictly to between 1 km and 100 km.
  - Unmapped PIN codes: If a newly introduced PIN code is not mapped in the portal's directory, manual entry is accepted up to a strict ceiling of 4,000 km.

## 5. E-Way Bill Validity Periods by Distance (Distance-Based Validity Framework)
- Standard Cargo (Regular Vehicles, Rail, Air, Ship):
  - Up to 200 km: Validity is 1 day for distance up to 200 km.
  - For every additional 200 km or part thereof: Additional 1 day validity is granted. (Rule revised from the historical 100 km/day to 200 km/day to align with national highway modernization).
- Over Dimensional Cargo (ODC) & Multimodal Vessel Shipments:
  - Covers ODC under Motor Vehicle Rules or multimodal transit where at least one leg involves sea/inland water vessel.
  - Up to 20 km: Validity is 1 day.
  - For every additional 20 km or part thereof: Additional 1 day validity is granted.
- Validity timeline calculation:
  - The clock begins at the timestamp when Part-B is entered (or immediately upon generating the full EWB).
  - Midnight reckoning: The first "day" expires at midnight of the day immediately following the date of E-Way Bill generation. (Example: An EWB generated at 3:00 PM on 10th October for 150 km expires at 12:00 midnight of 11th/12th October).
- Extension window (Rule 138(10)):
  - Permitted due to transit delays caused by vehicle breakdown, accidents, transshipment logjams, natural calamities, or force majeure.
  - Strict time window: Can be extended only within 8 hours before or 8 hours after the exact time of expiry.
  - Transporter must update Part-B (new vehicle or updated reason) and enter the remaining distance to calculate additional validity.

## 6. Vehicle Updates, Transshipment & Multi-Vehicle Movement
- Vehicle updation in Part-B (Form GST EWB-01): When a conveyance changes due to transshipment or mechanical breakdown, the generator or current transporter must update the new vehicle number on the portal prior to resuming movement.
- Vahan database integration:
  - The vehicle number format is validated in real time against the National Vahan vehicle registry.
  - Vehicle numbers not registered or mismatched with chassis/engine series in the Vahan database will be rejected or flagged.
- Transporter assignment (Change of Transporter):
  - The consignor/consignee can reassign the E-Way Bill to a new transporter using the Transporter ID (transporter_id).
  - The existing transporter can also reassign the EWB to another transporter for transit completion.
- Multi-Vehicle Movement (Split Consignment):
  - Used when a massive consignment or ODC load is transported across multiple individual trucks/trailers under a single tax invoice.
  - Workflow: The original E-Way Bill is updated on the portal under the "Multi-Vehicle" facility by entering the total quantity, units, and vehicle groups. Each distinct vehicle operates with its own specific transit acknowledgement slip containing the base EWB number.

## 7. Consolidated E-Way Bills (Form GST EWB-02)
- Purpose: For logistics operators transporting multiple consignments in a single conveyance, each having its own individual E-Way Bill.
- Generation: The transporter enters the conveyance details (vehicle number) and attaches all individual 12-digit E-Way Bill numbers to generate a single consolidated document (Form GST EWB-02).
- Validity rule: The consolidated E-Way Bill possesses a validity period equal to the shortest remaining validity among the linked individual E-Way Bills.

## 8. Statutory Exemptions from E-Way Bill (Rule 138(14))
No E-Way Bill is required under the following specific circumstances:
- Exempt Goods Annexure: Transport of goods specified in the Annexure to Rule 138(14) (e.g., LPG for domestic supply, postal baggage, curd, fresh fruits/vegetables, live animals, unbranded food grains, personal/used household effects).
- Precious metals: Natural or cultured pearls, precious or semi-precious stones, precious metals (Chapter 71), jewelry, and goldsmiths' wares (Note: Imitation jewelry under HSN 7117 is NOT exempt).
- Non-motorized conveyances: Movement of goods via animal carts, handcarts, cycle rickshaws, or manual head-loads.
- Customs transit: Movement from port, airport, air cargo complex, or land customs station to an Inland Container Depot (ICD) or Container Freight Station (CFS) for customs clearance.
- Under customs bond: Goods transported under customs supervision or customs seal from one customs station to another.
- Defense & Military cargo: Goods transported under military transit orders or by Ministry of Defence formations.
- Empty containers: Transit of empty cargo containers or empty LPG cylinders for reasons other than supply.
- Weighbridge transit: Movement up to 20 km from the consignor's place of business to a weighbridge and back, accompanied strictly by a delivery challan.

## 9. Recipient Acceptance / Rejection Facility (Rule 138(11) & (12))
- Statutory right: A recipient (buyer) linked to an E-Way Bill has the legal right to confirm or reject the transaction on the common portal.
- 72-hour window: The recipient must accept or reject the E-Way Bill within 72 hours of details being made available on the portal, or by the actual time of physical delivery of goods—whichever is earlier.
- Deemed acceptance: If no explicit rejection is registered within 72 hours, the portal automatically tags the document as Deemed Accepted.

## 10. E-Way Bill Cancellation Limits & Protocols
- 24-hour limit: An E-Way Bill can be cancelled by the generator within 24 hours of generation.
- Non-cancellation constraints:
  - An E-Way Bill cannot be cancelled if it has been intercepted and physically inspected by a GST verification officer in transit.
  - An E-Way Bill cannot be cancelled if its overall validity has completely expired.
- Irreversibility: Once cancelled, an E-Way Bill number is terminated and cannot be re-activated; if goods must still move, a fresh E-Way Bill must be generated.

## 11. Inspection, RFID Tracking & Interception in Transit
- Documents to be carried: The person-in-charge of a conveyance must carry:
  - The tax invoice, delivery challan, or bill of entry.
  - Physical copy of the E-Way Bill or the E-Way Bill number electronically mapped via RFID tag embedded on the vehicle.
- RFID tracking: Commercial vehicles moving through toll plazas equipped with Fastag/RFID readers automatically feed movement logs into the GST system, verifying vehicle transit paths against declared EWB routes.
- Summary inspection report (Part A - Form GST EWB-03): An intercepting proper officer must record an online summary report of the inspection within 24 hours of interception.
- Final inspection report (Part B - Form GST EWB-03): The final physical verification report must be uploaded by the officer within 3 days (72 hours) of the inspection.
- Vehicle detention reporting by transporter (Form GST EWB-04): If a vehicle is intercepted and detained by tax authorities for a period exceeding 30 minutes, the transporter has the statutory right to upload details of the detention on the common portal.

## 12. Penalties & Enforcement Consequences (Sections 122 & 129)
- Non-compliance penalty (Section 122): Transporting goods without a valid E-Way Bill attracts a penalty equal to 100% of the tax due or ₹10,000, whichever is higher.
- Detention and seizure (Section 129):
  - Taxable goods: Goods and conveyance are detained; release mandates payment of a penalty equal to 200% of the tax payable on the goods.
  - Exempted goods: Penalty equal to 2% of the value of goods or ₹25,000, whichever is lower.
- Confiscation (Section 130): If penalties under Section 129 are not paid within 14 days of detention, authorities may initiate confiscation proceedings against both the cargo and the transporting conveyance.
- Minor clerical errors (Circular No. 64/33/2018-GST): For trivial clerical errors where there is no intent to evade tax (e.g., spelling errors in consignor/consignee name, 1 or 2-digit error in vehicle number, error in HSN pin-code when address is correct), goods shall not be detained; a nominal penalty of ₹500 under CGST and ₹500 under SGST (Total ₹1,000) is levied under Section 125.

## 13. Integration with E-Invoicing System
- Unified generation: For taxpayers governed by the E-Invoicing mandate (AATO > ₹5 Crore), E-Way Bill Part-A details are directly auto-populated from the e-invoice payload (EwbDet) submitted to the Invoice Registration Portal (IRP).
- Decoupled generation: The taxpayer can obtain the IRN first and subsequently generate the linked E-Way Bill directly using the IRN on the E-Way Bill portal without re-entering invoice data.
- Auto-cancellation link: Cancelling an e-invoice on the IRP within 24 hours requires the linked E-Way Bill to be cancelled first; an active E-Way Bill prevents e-invoice cancellation.
